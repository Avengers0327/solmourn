# RUNNING SOLMOURN — A FIRST-TIME DM FIELD GUIDE

> **DM ONLY.** Your practical operating manual for running eight enjoyable sessions, even if you've never DMed before. Designed to be readable on a phone between scenes.

## The job in one paragraph

You're not writing a novel and making your friends act it out. You're presenting a situation, asking what their characters do, describing the world's response, and applying fair rules where outcomes are uncertain. In Solmourn, your job is to make the dying artificial sun, the city's people, and the players' decisions feel real—**not** to force the reveals in a predetermined order if the players choose a different route.

## The fastest possible rules reference

When someone attempts something risky:

1. **Ask intent and method.** “What are you trying to accomplish, and how?”
2. **Decide whether a roll is needed.** Don't roll for obvious facts, ordinary actions, or clues required to move the story.
3. **Pick the ability, skill/tool, and DC.** 2014-style rule of thumb: DC **10** easy, **15** moderate, **20** hard (DC 13 fits a smaller intermediate challenge).
4. **Roll d20 + ability modifier + proficiency bonus** if proficient. Advantage = roll 2d20 take higher; disadvantage = take lower.
5. **Describe concrete consequences.** Failure might cost time, heat cells, trust or position, not simply “nothing happens.”

**Action economy:** On a combat turn, a character normally gets movement, one action, at most one bonus action if a feature permits it, and one reaction per round (reset at start of next turn). **Concentration:** usually only one concentration spell at a time; damage can require a CON save (DC 10 or half damage, whichever higher). **Opportunity attack:** normally reaction when a visible enemy leaves your reach, unless it Disengages or a feature says otherwise.

**Spellcasting caution:** A bonus-action spell in 2014 rules restricts other spells cast that same turn to a cantrip with casting time 1 action. RP does not remove this rule. **Amplification** is an extra cost/risk, not a second spell or free spell level.

**Attacks:** d20 + attack modifier against AC. On hit roll listed damage. **Saving throws:** target rolls d20 + applicable modifier against DC. **HP at 0:** unconscious, death saves on turns unless stabilized; three successes stabilize, three failures die, natural 20 restores 1 HP. Healing stabilizes/revives as specified. Check published rules for nuances, including massive damage.

## Before session zero: agreements and logistics

### Your 15-minute group conversation

Ask:
- **Tone:** grim, hopeful, political, scary, or a blend? What should never happen on-screen?
- **Lines and veils:** content excluded entirely vs occurring only offscreen. Relevant subjects: poverty, starvation, frostbite, medical horror, imprisonment, surveillance, children in danger.
- **PvP:** default is no attacks/theft/hostile social checks between PCs without everyone agreeing.
- **Phones:** fine for sheets and app, but avoid endless browsing while another player acts.
- **House rules:** explain that Solmourn classes and RP are provisional and may be tuned **between** sessions, openly.
- **Scheduling:** session duration, breaks, number of players, replacement for absences.

A simple **pause/reframe** agreement lets anyone request a change without explaining personal details. Never punish a player for using it.

## The 5–10–5 preparation method

Spend **5 minutes** reviewing the last session and NPC motives; **10 minutes** writing three likely scenes and the three clues that keep the story moving; **5 minutes** collecting stat blocks/maps/notes.

For larger episodes, take more time on encounters and revelations, but don't write scripts for every possible outcome. The minimum viable prep is:

```text
First image / first line:
What problem is visible right now?
Three clues (different sources):
Important NPC wants / fears:
Two plausible player approaches:
One avoidable danger and one hard choice:
What changes if they do nothing?
My 1-line ending hook:
```

## How to run the first hour

1. **Start in action:** Everflame flickers in Frostward, then a blackout. Ask each PC one quick question: “Where are you, and whom are you trying to help?”
2. **Spotlight every player:** Let Warden organize protection, Shade notice a suspicious access hatch, Machinist inspect regulator, Votary help freezing civilians, Strider identify a safe route, Resonant sense abnormal field.
3. **Name the stakes:** loss of heat, a count of 43 missing, Mara's brother Tomas.
4. **Offer a real decision:** help neighbors now, follow maintenance clue, or confront official responders. All lead to new information and consequences.
5. **Close the first hour with action:** site location, NPC conflict, rescue or unexpected clue.

### Read-aloud trick

Describe **one sense, one moving detail, one choice**. Example: “Condensation freezes inside your scarf. A warning lamp blinks orange under the street grate. The evacuation door is closing—what do you do?” Stop speaking and let players respond.

## Investigation without railroading

**Three-clue rule:** for every essential revelation, place three ways to find it. Not three skill checks on the same notebook. Use **different sources**: person, object, event, device, rumor, or environmental effect.

**Never gate the only lead behind a single roll.** If the players fail Investigation to read a log, they still find the station number, but perhaps trigger an alarm or lose time before retrieving detailed data.

**Be honest with clues:** the truth is that Everflame machinery is aging. Tomas can suspect conspiracy; distinguish what NPCs *believe* from what is *true*. Avoid retrofitting a sabotage twist just because players guess one.

**Maintain secret visibility:** Track each fact as `DM_ONLY`, `RUMOR`, `PLAYER_DISCOVERED` or `PUBLIC`. Move it to `PLAYER_DISCOVERED` only when actually revealed at the table.

## Improvisation and agency

When players propose an unplanned idea, use **“yes, and,” “yes, but,” or “no, because”** according to the fiction:

- “Yes, that access tunnel could work; it's much colder and takes longer.”
- “Yes, you can attempt to bluff Seren; what credential are you using?”
- “No, a first-level Radiance Pattern cannot power 12 million homes; however it can test whether the regulator is drawing energy.”

**Don't negate a clever plan to preserve a scripted NPC speech.** Move information to an appropriate alternate source. Preserve consequences and keep world facts consistent.

## Encounter pacing and challenge

Solmourn is not a combat-every-room campaign. Aim for a mixture of exploration, social scenes and danger. For a 3-hour session, **one meaningful combat** may be enough, especially for beginners.

**Building combat:** determine number of PCs and levels; use a 2014 DMG encounter-building calculator or XP thresholds, verify challenge ratings and action economy. Start easier than you think, especially with untested homebrew classes. Re-skin published low-CR stat blocks (security drones, scavengers, automated guardians) rather than inventing huge damage numbers.

**Dynamic combat:** state objective besides 'kill everything': rescue civilians, keep regulator powered 3 rounds, escape freezing room, capture a fleeing technician. Enemies can retreat, surrender, negotiate, or change objective.

**Initiative on your phone:** Name | AC | HP | Condition | Initiative; highlight active turn; don't hide an NPC's obvious behavior because its stat block has elaborate tactics.

**Adjusting fairly:** If an encounter is too difficult, introduce a justified escape route, environmental cover, enemy retreat or earlier-planted ally—not an unexplained victory. If too easy, let the party feel effective rather than secretly multiplying HP every time.

## Solmourn homebrew adjudication

### Resonance basics

A Pattern costs RP; the caster also needs legal spell level, action, range, components, concentration if applicable, and an Anchor as required. **No double-dipping:** if a character uses RP, they do not also spend a spell slot. The six disciplines restrict the actual *mechanism* of a spell.

**Amplified Overcasting:** choose one approved boost, pay extra RP, take unavoidable strain, roll CON against exhaustion; max two per long rest, all spell levels eligible. Be explicit about the target/area/range selected before rolling. Emergency borrowing is once/long rest and cannot stack with Amplification.

**First Concordance vs PC Concordance:** Eight original suns formed under singular planetary conditions at the same exact instant. A modern PC's successful emergency Concordance may shield a corridor or stabilize a device—not spontaneously create another sun. Tell the players the immediate fiction, not the secret cosmology, until its reveal.

### When a class feature is unclear

1. Read exact wording in `02_PLAYER_GUIDE.md`.
2. Identify its action type, range, uses, recovery and stacking behavior.
3. Compare to a roughly similar published 5e ability.
4. Make a fair **temporary** ruling, write it down, revisit after the session with player input.

**Open issue you should resolve before session 1:** Resonant spell catalogs are **not complete** for all disciplines. If a player picks an underpopulated discipline, create/approve enough appropriate Patterns to make their level-3 book legal and useful. Don't punish a player for choosing Echo or Form.

## NPCs who feel like people

Use a simple five-field sheet:

```text
NAME:
PUBLIC ROLE:
WHAT THEY WANT TODAY:
WHAT THEY FEAR:
ONE FACT THEY KNOW / ONE THING THEY GET WRONG:
```

For example, **Seren Vale** may want to prevent panic, fear that unrestricted information will kill people, know official blackout procedure, and wrongly assume unauthorized casters are the primary risk. **Mara Venn** wants Tomas found even if the party rejects her politics.

**Voices optional:** you do not need accents. Give each NPC a distinct urgency, vocabulary, and motive.

## Handling the big reveals

### Session 4: old New York

Let discovery happen through **evidence**: old maps, subway markings, media, structural match. Don't say “You realize it's New York because you rolled 17.” A roll can help interpret evidence; the evidence makes the revelation compelling. Give the players time to react.

### Sessions 5–6: outside cities / Ramapo Ridge

Don't force one school-room route. The historical archive can be on an old school server, an evacuated researcher drive or mountain relay backup. If players skip the school, preserve the discovery in another believable place. Real-world Mahwah geography is inspiration, not a promise of exact present-day cartographic preservation after seven centuries.

### Session 6: the Unbound

Don't announce anyone was secretly chosen at birth. The prophecy permits an **individual born Unattuned who learns** and a **collective achieving Unity**. The players choose whether the prophecy is trustworthy or politically useful.

### Session 8: the ending

Describe tradeoffs without telegraphing which ending the DM prefers. Let players build a hybrid. The story can end with an imperfect but honest survival plan. Give each PC a role in resolution.

## Common first-time DM problems

| Situation | Good response |
|---|---|
| “I don't know the rule.” | “For now we'll rule X; I'll check after the session.” Log it. |
| Players split party | Switch scenes every few minutes and give each side a clear choice. |
| A PC argues with every NPC | Let NPCs respond naturally; move on, and ask what the rest of party does. |
| Players ignore your lead | Ask what they want, make the world respond, introduce alternate clues organically. |
| Nobody talks | Present two concrete options, then invite a third. Ask one character directly what they notice or do. |
| One player dominates | “Let's hear from someone who hasn't acted yet.” |
| Players ask for a lore dump | Give what their character would know, plus a useful next question. |
| They attempt something impossible | Explain constraints and offer nearest feasible action, not an arbitrary 'no'. |
| Combat drags | Summarize enemy turns, announce who's next, use simple tactics, call a brief break. |
| They uncover a secret early | Reward cleverness; move forward rather than invalidating their discovery. |

## Phone-first DM setup (no laptop needed during sessions)

Keep **five pinned views** in your notes/app: `CURRENT SCENE`, `NPCS`, `CLUES`, `INITIATIVE`, `QUICK RULES`. Use short cards, large tap targets and a one-handed layout. Write **brief notes** during session; clean them up after. Keep offline copies of your story bible and player-safe guide.

**Recommended homescreen**:

```text
SOLMOURN / Session 03
[Start session] [Quick encounter] [New note]
Now: The ration hearing
Next: Technician witness
Clues: 2/3 found
Clocks: Everflame 2/6 · ORS 1/6 · Unrest 2/6
NPCs: Seren · Mara · Technician
[Reveal clue] [Advance clock] [End session]
```

## After every session — 10-minute debrief

- **Recap:** what happened, 3 sentences maximum.
- **World facts invented:** NPC names, what a door opens into, promises, locations—make continuity explicit.
- **Unresolved questions:** what the players intend to do next.
- **Secret tracker:** what was actually revealed and to which players.
- **Clock changes:** what caused them and whether players know the effects.
- **Player feedback:** ask “What was most fun? What dragged? What would you like more of?”
- **Next prep:** first scene, likely NPC, three clues, two alternative solutions, one complication.

## Your permission slip as DM

You are allowed to say “I need a moment.” You're allowed to look something up. You're allowed to revise a homebrew feature after explaining why. You're allowed to make mistakes, and you can correct them openly. What players need from you most is **fairness, clear stakes, meaningful choices and an interesting world that reacts to them**—not perfect accents, encyclopedic rules knowledge or a flawless plot.

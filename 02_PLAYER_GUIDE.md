# SOLMOURN: THE DYING LIGHT — PLAYER GUIDE

> **SAFE TO SHARE WITH PLAYERS.** Contains what an ordinary resident of Solmourn might know plus character rules. Avoid reading private DM files unless invited.
>
> **System:** D&D 5e (2014-based), with Solmourn homebrew. **Start:** level 3. **All PCs are human.** Class rules and spell lists are a **playtest draft**, and the DM will adjudicate edge cases fairly.

## Welcome to Solmourn

Your world is cold. Beyond the great city, darkness stretches over ruins and ice. Above Solmourn burns the **Everflame**, an artificial sun that provides light, heat, and the conditions necessary for millions to survive.

The **Solar Imperium** claims to protect the last great refuge of humanity. It issues energy rations, regulates dangerous technologies, and tightly controls personal magic. Solmourn's people do not agree about whether the Imperium is their salvation, their jailer, or both.

**Your campaign begins in Frostward.** An unexplained interruption in the city's warmth draws together people who would otherwise never cooperate.

### Tone and expectations

Investigation, survival, difficult choices, class tension, mystery, occasional horror, social negotiation, and tactical action. No species other than humans; no fantasy gods, automatic divine intervention, or standard fantasy assumptions. Your character can question authority, support it, ignore it, or change their mind. There is **no required 'good' faction**.

Light can be comforting and oppressive; darkness can be dangerous and liberating. Neither is morally good or evil by nature.

## What ordinary residents know

- The Everflame supports the city; its infrastructure is heavily protected.
- The city is divided into districts with vastly different access to resources.
- Most people use infrastructure that includes complex technology they do not fully understand.
- Some individuals can perform **Resonance** magic, though personal casting is rare and heavily regulated.
- Travel beyond the heated perimeter is perilous; rumors about the outside world are inconsistent and unreliable.
- The Solar Imperium maintains order and aggressively investigates unauthorized magic.

**You do not need to know the world's lost history to make a character.** In fact, discovering it is part of the adventure.

## The city's six districts

| District | A useful character angle |
|---|---|
| Everflame Sanctum | Highly restricted technical or security access; needs DM approval |
| Imperial Crown | Family ties to officials, servants, researchers or governing institutions |
| Gilded Ring | Education, trade, formal licenses and privilege |
| Inner Wards | Neighborhood networks, dense city life, transport work |
| Foundries | Engineering, heavy labor, maintenance and industrial knowledge |
| Frostward | Scarcity, mutual aid, smuggling, endurance and survival |

A PC may have *moved* districts. Birth district isn't destiny, and it does not determine whether someone is capable of magic.

## Character creation checklist

1. Choose **human** or **variant human** (2014 D&D 5e). Ask the DM to approve variant-human feat choices.
2. Assign scores with standard array **15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8** or 27-point buy.
3. Choose one of the **six Solmourn classes** below.
4. Choose a **district background** and its skills. Avoid duplicate skill proficiencies; replace duplicates by agreement with the DM.
5. Choose a personal **Attunement status**: Attuned to one discipline or Unattuned. Only the **Resonant class** starts with independent spellcasting. Being Attuned without training does **not** automatically grant spells.
6. Calculate level-3 hit points and features. Proficiency bonus is **+2** at level 3. Pick equipment.
7. Write: **one personal goal**, **one person you care about**, **one secret**, and **one reason you're in Frostward** during the opening scene.
8. Establish **one existing connection** to another PC. This is a team game—build a reason to cooperate.

### Quick example

**Rin** is a human Foundries Machinist (level 3), once disciplined for bypassing an energy meter. She wants to prevent Frostward heat cuts, has a relative in the Inner Wards, and knows the party's Warden from a factory accident. No secret prophecy knowledge is needed.

## Core 2014 rules still apply

AC, initiative, d20 attacks, advantage/disadvantage, proficiency, saving throws, concentration, death saves, movement, action/bonus action/reaction, short/long rests, and conditions work as in 2014 D&D 5e unless explicitly changed. **ASI** means ability score improvement (or a permitted feat). Class spellcasting uses RP, not extra spell slots.

At level 3: proficiency bonus +2. Ability modifier is (score − 10) / 2, rounded down. Use class starting HP at level 1 plus two level-ups or the standard fixed HP gain.

## The six disciplines — public magical knowledge

| Discipline | What it manipulates |
|---|---|
| **Radiance** | Light, heat, electricity, cold and energy |
| **Kinesis** | Force, movement, gravity and momentum |
| **Vitality** | Healing, anatomy, disease, poison and living systems |
| **Veil** | Illusions, perception, emotion and memory |
| **Form** | Matter, shapes, material properties and structure |
| **Echo** | Information, traces, detection and signals |

A trained caster usually works within **one** discipline. Spells are known as **Patterns**; a personal casting interface is an **Anchor**. Industrial devices may incorporate Resonance without being operated by a personal caster.

### RESONANT

**Hit die:** d6. **Saves:** INT/WIS. **Weapons:** Wizard weapon set; no armor by default. **Skills:** two from Arcana, History, Investigation, Insight, Medicine, Religion (as cultural studies). **Requirement:** Attuned to one discipline. **Casting ability:** Intelligence. **Spell attack:** INT + PB. **Save DC:** 8 + INT + PB.

**Patternbook:** six 1st-level Patterns at level 1, plus two legal new Patterns every Resonant level; preparation count = Resonant level + INT modifier (min 1). At campaign start level 3, your book has **10 Patterns**, your maximum Pattern level is **2**, and you have **14 RP**, **three cantrips**, and a personal Anchor. The DM must supply enough suitable Patterns in your discipline to fill the book; duplicates cannot be used to fake progress.

- **Medical Training (1):** Medicine Expertise.

- **Beacon of Humanity (20):** once/long rest action, six allies within 30 ft gain 15 temp HP and end frightened.

### Amplified Overcasting (unlocked for trained Resonants)

Any Pattern—even a cantrip or level-9 Pattern—may be amplified at casting, even when you have sufficient RP. You may use Amplification **twice per long rest**.

| Tier | Extra RP | Unavoidable strain damage | CON save to avoid 1 exhaustion |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| +1 | +2 | 1d6 | DC 12 |
| +2 | +4 | 2d6 | DC 15 |
| +3 | +6 | 4d6 | DC 18 |
| +4 | +8 | 6d6 | DC 21 |

Choose **one** fitting enhancement: one target's damage +1 spell-damage die/tier (even if area spell); healing +1d8/tier to one healing roll; range ×2/tier; duration ×2/tier (max 24 h, concentration remains); area +5 ft radius/tier or +10 ft line/cone per tier; or +1 spell-save DC per 2 tiers, max +2. Special Patterns may replace generic bonuses. This never adds a whole new power, bypasses materials, removes concentration, or grants unlimited energy.

**Emergency Overcasting:** once/long rest, borrow up to **2 missing RP**. Take unavoidable **1d6 force per borrowed RP**, CON save DC **10 + 2 × borrowed RP** to avoid 1 exhaustion. Cannot combine with Amplification or bypass your highest Pattern level.

## Equipment and credit rules

Use published 2014 statistics, with Solmourn presentation:

| 5e equipment | In-world equivalent |
|---|---|
| Leather armor | Reinforced insulated coat |
| Studded leather | Composite survival vest |
| Chain shirt | Industrial mesh armor |
| Plate | Heavy Imperial exoshell; **does not grant powered-armor bonuses** |
| Longbow/crossbow | Mechanical hunting bow/bolt launcher |
| Healing potion | Sealed medical injector (same standard 5e effect) |
| Arcane focus | Personal Anchor |

Each level-3 PC starts with class equipment, cold-weather clothing, 3 days of rations, basic water supply, a district ID or reasonable substitute, and **20 credits**. For playtesting prices, 1 credit = 1 gp for mundane equipment; this is **not** the settled economy. Advanced guns, unlimited power cells, exosuit boosts and military weapons are not ordinary starting items.

### Class starting loadouts

- **Warden:** shield, one melee weapon, one ranged option, medium armor or reinforced coat, survival pack.
- **Shade:** reinforced coat, two light weapons, thieves' tools, rope, one disguise kit or forgery kit.
- **Machinist:** medium armor, toolkit, Field Rig with two prepared devices, simple weapon, spare parts pouch.
- **Resonant:** Anchor, Patternbook, light crossbow or quarterstaff, research instruments, insulated clothes.
- **Votary:** reinforced coat, 10-use healer's kit, medical satchel, simple weapon, shelter supplies.
- **Strider:** medium armor or reinforced coat, bow or spear, climbing gear, snow goggles, survival pack.

## Background choices

2014 background format: 2 skill proficiencies, 2 tool/language proficiencies, a roleplaying feature, and starting flavor items. Language choices represent the surviving human languages/trade codes, not fantasy languages.

| Background | Skills | Contacts/perk (narrative, not automatic victory) |
|---|---|---|
| Crown Scion | History, Insight | Etiquette and access to some privileged society |
| Gilded Citizen | Persuasion, Investigation | Commercial credentials and licensed contacts |
| Ward Resident | Insight, Perception | Shelter and local neighborhood rumor network |
| Foundry Worker | Athletics, Investigation | Trade knowledge, union/coworker contacts, workshop access |
| Frostward Survivor | Survival, Stealth | Mutual aid and improvised shelter contacts |

Choose two fitting tool/language proficiencies with the DM. District background does not force loyalty to the Imperium.

## Team play and safety

- Make a character who can cooperate with the party even when they disagree.
- Ask about lines and veils (topics excluded entirely vs handled offscreen), especially cold exposure, deprivation, coercion, and body horror.
- Ask questions. You can attempt unexpected solutions. The DM will tell you what your character can plausibly know and what check—if any—is required.
- Keep a simple session note: **what we learned / what we want / whom we trust / questions remaining**.

### What you're not supposed to know yet

Not everything the Imperium teaches is true. Some parts of history and magic are deliberately mysterious. **Don't seek out private DM material; start with what your character genuinely knows.**

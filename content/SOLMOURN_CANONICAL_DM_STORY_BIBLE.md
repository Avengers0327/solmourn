# 0. How to use this bible

## 0.1 Authority and canon status

- **LOCKED** — The user expressly selected or accepted this as established setting/campaign canon. Do not alter without permission.
- **WORKING** — An offered name, number, explanation, or design that has not been individually approved. Useful for prep; never present as immutable history.
- **OPEN** — Intentionally undecided or dependent on the players. Do not invent a definitive answer.
- **RETIRED** — Earlier statements that conflict with later canon; do not reintroduce them.

**Precedence:** (1) the user's explicit future decisions; (2) this bible's LOCKED statements; (3) this bible's WORKING statements, clearly labeled; (4) older handoff documents as *implementation references only*; (5) improvisation, recorded as new working material until approved. If an apparent contradiction remains, flag it instead of silently resolving it.

## 0.2 Campaign identity [LOCKED]

- Title: **Solmourn: The Dying Light**.
- Genre: post-apocalyptic Earth, dark science fantasy, high-density urban exploration, political repression, survival engineering, mystery, and meaningful choices.
- **Fifteen sessions** total; **branching narrative** (option B): key crises and discoveries have planned windows, but alliances, approaches, missions, consequences, and ending depend on players.
- Intended for a **first-time DM** and players who especially enjoy **combat and exploration**.
- Rules foundation: D&D 5e-inspired, with a mixed-edition/homebrew approach; humans only; character concepts mapped to custom classes; spell-point/roleplay-forward Resonance rather than traditional 5e spell slots.
- Players are not forced to join the Cinders, oppose the Imperium, become Unbound, or select any predetermined ending.

## 0.3 DM safety and reveal discipline [LOCKED]

- This document contains facts unknown to virtually all in-world characters. **DM knowledge ≠ NPC knowledge ≠ player knowledge.**
- Do not expose the other Embers, New York identity, ancient geography, Concordance, or Unbound truth in opening-session UI, search, handouts, notifications, previews, exports, or AI-generated player recaps.
- Use explicitly approved **player-safe summaries** for sharing. Revealing one clue never exposes the private entry behind it.
- Players may discover things earlier or later through extraordinary play, but the default dramatic reveal schedule must not leak accidentally.

# 1. The truth of the world (DM ONLY)

## 1.1 Earth and the Sundering [LOCKED]

This is **Earth**, not a separate fantasy world. Humanity developed Resonance in the late 21st/22nd-century future (working historical date **~2180 CE**). Scientific, corporate, and state experimentation with Resonance eventually created **Dissonance**, a planet-scale physical interference phenomenon. Around **2230 CE**, the **Sundering** occurred. The natural sun **was not destroyed**: it remains in the sky, but Dissonance prevents its normal light and heat from reaching the planet's surface.

The world became lethally cold. Civilization collapsed across most of Earth. Darkness is a physical condition, **not** a morally evil substance, a god, demon, monster, or default source of corruption.

The present is approximately **700 AS (After Sundering)**, about seven centuries after the catastrophe; using ~2230 CE as the historical approximation gives a rough equivalent of **~2930 CE**. Do not confuse this with merely 700 years after 2026.

## 1.2 The nine first lights [LOCKED; DM ONLY]

In the original catastrophe, **nine separate groups** of desperate humans, at nine geographically distant locations, unknowingly achieved a simultaneous, emergent alignment of Resonance. This event created **nine artificial suns**. Their participants did not possess a learned recipe; they were not consciously casting a documented group spell. Later engineers developed machinery to **stabilize and maintain** the suns. Technology did not retroactively create the initial suns.

The phenomenon behind their creation is **Concordance**. The term is DM terminology for the lost truth, **not** common in-world vocabulary. Some of the original founders became ruling or influential elites, but modern political systems diverged; not every Ember became a monarchy.

**Concordance is completely lost as a usable art.** Nobody alive in the current era—Seraphine, ORS, Cinders, the Vigil, Aureth's scholars, or foreign rulers—knows how to enact it. No extant spellbook, device, secret cult, archival manual, or knowledgeable mentor can simply teach it.

## 1.3 Concordance and Dissonance [LOCKED; DM ONLY]

- **Dissonance:** large-scale disruptive Resonance interference responsible for the Sun's light/heat failing to reach Earth. Not a creature or evil force with intentions.
- **Concordance:** rare, emergent group harmony among Resonance participants that can produce effects unavailable to their separate powers. Its original emergence was spontaneous and crisis-born.
- **False Dawns:** historical brief warmth/light bursts, some caused by failed spontaneous Concordances. People in the present do not know their true cause.
- **Restoration:** the party may eventually discover a way to save the Everflame and/or restore the natural sun's light; no method is preordained or guaranteed. Engineering, human choice, sacrifice, and political consequences matter.

The party's discovery should emerge from observation, experimentation, interaction, and meaningful risk around **sessions 10–12**, not from a convenient exposition dump.

## 1.4 The Unbound prophecy [LOCKED; DM ONLY]

A suppressed/obscure prophecy concerns a future learned Resonant and/or group able to unify the six disciplines. **Unbound** may ultimately refer to one individual or a collective achievement; the answer is **OPEN**, determined by the story and players. The prophecy's possibilities include restoration of the natural sun or the Everflame. Do not designate a chosen PC in advance, compel an awakening, or imply that dark/light maps to evil/good.

The precise wording, origins, reliability, and public circulation of the prophecy remain **OPEN**. Never build an app feature that assigns the Unbound identity automatically.

# 2. The Nine Embers

## 2.1 Names, locations, political structures [LOCKED]

The nine **Embers** are the original artificial-sun cities. **Embers** is the accepted collective term. Do not call them “havens” or “pyres.” The city names and former locations below are DM-only historical truth. Political types were finalized in Pass 3A; exact leaders, constitutions, and current survival status outside Solmourn remain largely open.

| Ember | Former geography | Government identity | Distinctive orientation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Solmourn** | New York City, USA | Absolute hereditary monarchy, House Vael | Thermal megainfrastructure, surveillance, elite military exoshells |
| **Vesperdeep** | Reykjavík, Iceland | Technocratic federation | Geothermal redundancy and deep-earth engineering |
| **Kharos** | Cairo, Egypt | **Military dictatorship** | Fortification, military logistics, managed water and agriculture |
| **Aureth** | Paris, France | Scholarly republic | Archives, research, precision engineering |
| **Namaris** | Mumbai, India | Mercantile federation | Manufacturing, recycling, logistics, guild/cooperative bargaining |
| **Sevrin** | Seoul, South Korea | Technocratic civic state | Robotics, autonomous maintenance, algorithm-assisted planning |
| **Orrakai** | Tokyo, Japan | Arcology-based civic federation | Vertical urbanism, integrated infrastructure, local councils |
| **Caldris** | São Paulo, Brazil | Ecological commonwealth | Biotechnology, closed-loop agriculture, regenerative systems |
| **Azmara** | Lagos, Nigeria | Decentralized district federation | Modular infrastructure, local adaptation, distributed systems |

**Interpretation:** These governments are their finalized political identities in the setting. The precise institutions, current incumbents, internal factions, and whether each still functions unchanged at 700 AS are **OPEN**. Distinctive technical specialties are accepted worldbuilding baselines, not quantified claims of superiority.

## 2.2 Aureth's near-discovery [LOCKED]

A tiny circle of Aureth researchers suspects that **some unidentified collective Resonance phenomenon** might explain scattered anomalous observations. They cannot prove it, reproduce it, name it as Concordance, or connect it to the original creation of the nine Embers. Most citizens are unaware; mainstream researchers are skeptical. Their theories are incomplete and often wrong.

**Never write:** “Aureth knows Concordance,” “Aureth has Concordance technology,” or “Aureth can teach the party Concordance.” They have only **theoretical suspicion**, option **B**.

## 2.3 Contact and isolation [LOCKED]

The Embers once had limited communications and surveys. A historical survey around **10 AS** mapped the original nine and pre-Sundering geography. **Solmourn deliberately severed outside communications/trade around 50 AS**, earlier than the general collapse of inter-Ember contact elsewhere around **300–400 AS**.

By 700 AS, Solmourn's ordinary people, current Imperial leadership, and the Cinders **do not know that the other Embers exist**. Old records are fragmentary, suppressed, lost, or no longer interpreted correctly. This includes Seraphine: she must not secretly have an active foreign diplomatic channel or a complete nine-city intelligence dossier.

The existence of foreign civilizations does not guarantee they are thriving, friendly, contactable, or politically unchanged. Any later contact is an earned, uncertain possibility, not an automatic rescue fleet.

## 2.4 Survival outside the nine [LOCKED]

Other human communities survived outside the original Embers through geothermal, underground, nuclear, or other infrastructure, including possible East African Rift settlements. They are **not additional original Embers**. The open-air **Wastes** beyond functioning thermal protection are largely barren and lethally cold; do not populate them by default with thriving villages, monster hordes, or ordinary wilderness ecosystems.

# 3. Solmourn: geography, population, and urban life

## 3.1 Geographic identity [LOCKED; DM ONLY until reveal]

Solmourn is built upon and through the ruins of **New York City**. The Everflame hangs above former **Midtown Manhattan**. Its inhabited built environment spreads irregularly to roughly **55 km** at its outermost extent, reaching the **Mahwah/Ramapo, New Jersey** region. The geography is not a geometrically perfect set of rings; the rings are administrative and thermal-distance bands.

The identity of Manhattan and New York is revealed through ancient signage, maps, and exploration, **not** stated to players at campaign start. Potential fragments include “42 ST,” partial park and rail signage, and old map overlays.

## 3.2 Six rings [LOCKED]

| Ring | Approximate radius from Everflame | Population | Defining characteristics |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Sanctum** | 0–0.7 km | **8,000** | Sacred/technical Everflame Core, Basilica, restricted personnel, pilgrimage access; not a normal city residential zone |
| **Crown** | 0.7–2.5 km | **392,000** | Palace, dynasty, ministries, high nobility, redundancy and privilege |
| **Gilded Ring** | 2.5–5 km | **1,600,000** | Wealth, academies, licensed Resonance, high-service housing |
| **Inner Wards** | 5–12 km | **7,000,000** | Dense mixed commerce, schools, hospitals, working/middle-class neighborhoods |
| **Foundries** | 12–25 km | **15,000,000** | Factories, industrial housing, constructs, freight and maintenance |
| **Frostward** | 25–55 km | **30,000,000** | Extremely dense outer-city tenements, community networks, unreliable heating |
| **TOTAL** | — | **54,000,000** | |

**Frostward ≠ Wastes.** Frostward is a densely populated urban ring with millions of residents, high-rises, transit, shops, and neighborhoods. The Wastes begin beyond functioning thermal protection.

## 3.3 Ringism [LOCKED]

Ringism combines **institutional discrimination** and **social prejudice** (user selection **B + A**). People are nominally legally equal, but ring of birth/residence influences heating priorities, schools, employment, licensing, transit, surveillance, and access to justice. It is **not** a formal hereditary caste system, a biological human difference, or a rule that nobody can move between rings.

Frostward residents are not interchangeable victims: they maintain families, businesses, cultural life, pride, loyalties, political disagreements, and mutual aid. Some genuinely support the Imperium; others resent it or simply try to survive.

## 3.4 Infrastructure baseline [LOCKED as established world model; numerical engineering details OPEN]

**Housing:** Vertical high-density towers, stacked neighborhoods, subsurface districts, elevated walkways, converted old structures, and integrated commercial levels. Inner rings enjoy more reliable maintenance and living space; Frostward has crowding and improvised repairs.

**Heat:** Everflame energy is captured and distributed through **thermal capture stations**, primary insulated **heat arteries**, district exchangers, local loops, storage reservoirs, and heat-recovery systems. Radiant warmth alone does not heat the whole megacity. Thermal loss, old equipment, lack of redundancy, and politically unequal maintenance make Frostward vulnerable. The Crown and Sanctum have prioritized redundant service.

**Food:** Controlled-environment hydroponic/aeroponic farms, microbial fermentation, fungal/algae systems, and limited cultivated animal products. Ministry of Provision oversees essential reserves; licensed private and cooperative producers also operate. The city is not casually dependent on open-air farming or routine imports from other Embers.

**Water:** High-recovery wastewater reclamation, condensation, managed ice extraction, reserves, filtration, and industrial recycling. Poorer neighborhoods can face pressure failures and contamination.

**Transport:** High-capacity trunk rail, district metro, vertical lifts/funiculars, freight rail, pedestrian skyways, and specialized ground vehicles. Aerial vehicles are limited and specialized; ordinary mass travel is not flying cars. Ring checkpoints and inequality affect journeys.

**Electricity:** Everflame energy conversion, distribution, storage, waste-heat recovery, and limited backup generation; separate but interconnected with the thermal network. Hospitals, agriculture, transit, and industry are energy-intensive.

**Communications:** Sophisticated regulated local digital networks and computing. The ORS monitors politically sensitive activity but is not magically omniscient. The Cinders use clandestine local communications and couriers.

**Economy:** **State-directed mixed economy**: Imperial control of critical Core and primary thermal infrastructure; public oversight of water and staples; private, guild, cooperative, and state enterprises in other sectors; wages, markets, licensing, taxation, ration guarantees, informal economies.

**Healthcare and education:** Technologically advanced but unequally accessible; prosthetics, automated diagnostics, and some Vitality-assisted care. Basic education exists widely; elite access and opportunities are skewed toward inner rings.

**Culture:** Music, theater, sports, games, restaurants, festivals, gardens, family life, local traditions, and humor persist. The Imperium represses political dissent; it does not eliminate all ordinary joy.

**Critical realism rule:** All this requires immense energy, material recycling, maintenance, workforce capacity, and governance. If an adventure depends on exact production capacity, establish and record a **WORKING** engineering estimate; do not pretend the above establishes exact megawatts, agricultural yields, transit speeds, or repair times.

# 4. The Everflame and the failing city

## 4.1 What the Everflame is [LOCKED]

The Everflame is the artificial sun above Midtown and the foundation of Solmourn's survival. Its initial manifestation came from emergent Concordance during the Sundering. Later machinery stabilized its output and enabled thermal/electrical capture and distribution.

**It is failing for real engineering reasons:** centuries-old stabilization systems, deferred replacement, degraded components, difficult energy balancing, and infrastructure stresses. **It is not secretly sabotaged, cursed, or powered by an evil entity.** Imperial engineers understand many mechanisms but not enough to fabricate an equivalent replacement sun from scratch.

## 4.2 Political allocation [LOCKED]

As the Everflame weakens, the Imperium diverts resources to the Core and prioritized districts. Repairs, storage, emergency shedding, and rationing can delay disaster but not indefinitely reverse it. This creates real ethical and political choices: technical necessity may be genuine, while **which communities are sacrificed** is a policy decision.

Seraphine can be both competent and monstrously unjust. Do not simplify the crisis into “the regime invented the emergency” or “every atrocity was unavoidable.”

## 4.3 Endgame solutions [OPEN]

The party may attempt to repair or transform the Everflame, restore access to the natural sun, bargain with or challenge the Imperium, or pursue another grounded solution discovered through play. The mechanics, costs, casualties, and final outcome must not be predetermined in the Story Bible. No button, NPC, artifact, or foreign Ember should solve it automatically.

# 5. Resonance: magic and technology

## 5.1 Six disciplines [LOCKED]

| Discipline | Domain | Typical expressions |
|---|---|---|
| **Radiance** | Light, heat, electricity | Illumination, thermal effects, electrical discharges |
| **Kinesis** | Force and motion | Push, pull, barriers, kinetic manipulation |
| **Vitality** | Biology and healing | Wound care, bodily enhancement, biological change |
| **Veil** | Perception and memory | Illusion, misdirection, altered perception |
| **Form** | Matter and structure | Shaping, reinforcement, material change |
| **Echo** | Signals, information, traces | Detection, communication, residual impressions |

Resonance is a rare but real physical/magical discipline, integrated with advanced technology. The Imperium regulates or suppresses it differently by ring. Crown institutions use it extensively; Gilded use is licensed; Inner Wards know fragments; many Foundries/Frostward citizens consider it distant, rare, or mythical.

## 5.2 Aptitude, training, and exceptional cases [LOCKED]

- Innate Attuned are rare: approximately **1 in 1,000** (worldbuilding prevalence, not a guaranteed exact census).
- Learned awakening without innate aptitude is **exceptionally rare** and significant to the prophecy.
- Ordinary Resonants generally master **one discipline**; mastering all six is exceptional and linked to the **Unbound** possibility.
- Overcasting can amplify effects at a physical cost. It is dangerous, not free narrative magic.
- Human-only character creation; no fantasy racial spellcasting, gods, or divine magic as default explanations.

## 5.3 Spell-point rules: preserve provisional status [WORKING / PLAYTEST]

The older app handoff describes **Resonance Points (RP)**, spell-like **Patterns**, **Amplification**, and **Emergency Overcasting**. The broad preference for spell points and overcasting is **LOCKED**; the detailed numeric rulebook has **not** been fully ratified in the later worldbuilding conversation.

Legacy implementation examples to retain as **PLAYTEST**, not immutable lore:

- Cantrips can cost **0 RP**.
- A level-2 Pattern costs **3 RP** in the referenced legacy test.
- Legacy example: level-3 Resonant has **14 RP**.
- Legacy example: +2 Amplification adds **4 RP**, **2d6 strain**, and a **DC 15** Constitution check, consuming one of two uses.
- Legacy Emergency Overcasting: borrow at most **2 RP**; take **1d6 damage per borrowed RP**; Constitution DC **10 + 2 × borrowed RP**; cannot stack with Amplification.
- Legacy high-level Patterns 6–9 have a once-per-long-rest ceiling.

These are **source-specific playtest rules** to audit against the latest class guide and user decisions, not an invitation to fill a spell directory by making up official 5e text. Published D&D spells may be mapped by **name, level, source, and discipline**; provide original descriptions/mechanics only for genuinely original or otherwise licensed content. Spell balance, access levels, complete class progression, and custom Pattern list remain **OPEN** until approved.

# 6. The Solar Imperium

## 6.1 Absolute sovereignty [LOCKED]

Solmourn is an **absolute hereditary monarchy**, not a constitutional monarchy. The Empress is the state: supreme legislative, executive, military, judicial, security, emergency, and dynastic authority. Ministers, generals, nobles, clerics, and technical advisers **cannot veto** her lawful sovereign commands. She can remove leaders, override courts, seize property, direct military operations, and order executions.

Her power is **legally absolute but physically constrained** by logistics, energy, incomplete intelligence, engineering, political consequences, and human limits. The setting should make her terrifying and formidable, not magically omnipotent or foolishly theatrical.

## 6.2 Empress Seraphine I Vael [LOCKED]

Seraphine is the most feared person in Solmourn. Her primary identity is an **Imperial Strategist** (user chose B), not a berserker or strongest duelist. She is calm, calculating, disciplined, politically ambitious, and frighteningly competent. She genuinely wants humanity to survive **and** believes that survival must remain under **House Vael**. She is capable of calculated atrocities and may try to appropriate any solution the party discovers.

**Character anchor:** “Mercy is mine to grant. It is not yours to presume.”

She should not be reframed as a misunderstood benevolent administrator. Her rationality does not absolve her cruelty. Her weaknesses include dynastic pride, overcentralization, frightened subordinates distorting intelligence, dependence on failing infrastructure, and underestimating cooperation beyond her control.

**WORKING biographical numbers:** age ~52; reign ~677–700 AS (23 years). Do not hard-code these as approved dates without confirmation.

## 6.3 House Vael [LOCKED principle; WORKING roster]

The dynasty is **united** (user chose A): no convenient secret rebel princess, easy internal coup, or sibling whose purpose is to overthrow Seraphine. Members may disagree about methods but support the dynasty's supremacy. Succession is hereditary under the Continuity Doctrine; no parliament or regency council possesses independent sovereign authority.

**WORKING names/details:** Crown Princess **Aurelia Vael** (~26), capable loyal reformer within monarchy; Prince **Lucien Vael** (~22), loyal militarist; Lord **Hadrian Vael** (~74), loyal isolationist elder. Their names/ages are proposals until expressly locked.

## 6.4 Government organs [LOCKED structure; WORKING individual names]

- **Imperial Chancery:** decrees, appointments, records, administration.
- **Thermal Stewardship:** heat allocations, grid maintenance, emergency engineering.
- **Ministry of Provision:** food, water, essential reserves.
- **Ministry of Works:** construction, transit, industrial maintenance.
- **Treasury:** finance, taxation, procurement.
- **Imperial High Command:** Legions and conventional defense.
- **Office of Resonant Security (ORS):** political intelligence, counterinsurgency, Resonant regulation, repression.
- **Ring Prefects / District Constabularies:** local administration and policing.
- **Sanctum Guard:** Core protection and critical-site security.
- **Solar Ascendancy:** hereditary elite warrior houses.

All answer to Seraphine. Separate operational chains must not be mistaken for a power-sharing constitution.

**WORKING named officials:** Dr. Cassian Veyr (Chief Thermal Architect), General Ilyra Voss (High Marshal), Malrec Thane (ORS Director), Lady Mirelle Saren (Chancellor), Mother Elian Corven (First Luminary).

## 6.5 Repression doctrine [LOCKED]

The user selected **A + B**:

1. **Surgical terror:** targeted surveillance, infiltration, arrests, disappearances, political trials, executions, and intimidation.
2. **Collective retribution:** when authorities establish or believe they have established support networks, they may punish families, workplaces, organizations, and neighborhoods via seizures, revoked privileges, relocation, restricted services, and occupation.

This is not mindless citywide random violence as a default. Collective punishment is often unjust and disproportionate, especially in Frostward. Investigations can be mistaken; the ORS cannot know everything. The Empress need not follow a scripted escalation ladder and may order exceptional measures directly.

## 6.6 Legitimacy and propaganda [LOCKED]

User selected **C + D**: a **divided empire** plus a **cult of the sovereign**. Public loyalty is expected, but private belief ranges from sincere admiration to fear, pragmatism, and anger. Crown/Gilded support is generally stronger; Foundries/Inner Wards are mixed; Frostward is not uniformly rebellious or loyal.

Dynastic portraits, civic oaths, broadcasts, schools, transit, and ceremonies portray House Vael as humanity's indispensable protector. Seraphine is **not literally divine**. Suggested slogan: “Seven centuries of darkness. Seven centuries of survival. One unbroken sovereign line.”

# 7. The Vigil of the First Light

## 7.1 Faith and state [LOCKED]

The **Vigil of the First Light** is a state-sponsored religion/civic faith focused on the Everflame. The Imperium is **not a theocracy**. The artificial sun is human-made, not a god; there is no default divine spellcasting. Some adherents believe it is sacred or conscious; others regard the rites as symbolic. The Empress is politically supreme and can remove religious leaders.

The hierarchy includes First Luminary, High Keepers, Keepers, and Attendants. Orthodox elements tend to support the regime; reformist currents argue that warmth belongs to all. Working reform label: **Open Flame**. Worship is not universally mandatory; political opposition remains dangerous.

## 7.2 The nine-rayed disk [LOCKED — ESSENTIAL FORESHADOWING]

The Vigil's sacred symbol is a **golden disk with NINE rays**. This is deliberate and essential. The Vigil arose **before Solmourn isolated around 50 AS**. The original nine rays referenced the **nine Embers**; centuries later the meaning was forgotten and reinterpreted as virtues or sacred qualities.

**Nobody in present-day Solmourn understands the original significance.** The player-facing symbol should appear naturally in churches, banners, uniforms, architecture, and ritual objects long before the map reveal. At sessions 8–9, perceptive players may connect the nine rays to the nine cities. **Do not explain it prematurely.**

## 7.3 Important places [LOCKED baseline]

The Sanctum contains the **Great Basilica**, **Hall of First Light**, **Chamber of Vigil**, Core machinery, and guarded restricted zones. The Hall may contain archives, but **not** a convenient Concordance manual or living keeper of the secret.

**WORKING holiday names:** First Illumination, Keeping, Long Vigil.

# 8. Imperial armed and security power

## 8.1 Technological level [LOCKED]

User selected **B + C**: **science-fantasy military supremacy** with **near-mythic capability** concentrated among top Ascendants and select sovereign assets. This is a civilization ~700 years after a 2230-era catastrophe, not a modern army with decorative armor. Advanced systems are plausible within the fictional technological history, while energy, materials, maintenance, expertise, and fragile infrastructure still constrain their deployment.

## 8.2 Solar Ascendancy [LOCKED identity and role]

The Ascendancy consists of **hereditary warrior houses** (user selected A). Its members receive generations of elite training, wealth, equipment, and institutional support. They are **not genetically superior humans**. Their powered exoshells, energy weapons, mobility systems, sensors, Resonance conduits, defensive fields, and autonomous support can make exceptional individuals devastating. Even the strongest Ascendant fears Seraphine's political authority.

**WORKING five-house roster (not yet individually approved):**

|---|---|
| **Varkhos** | Heavy defense / Bastion |
| **Caelith** | Rapid strikes / Spear |
| **Noxmere** | Reconnaissance / Veil |
| **Solvaris** | Siege / Hammer |
| **Aurelian** | Close combat / Radiant Blade |

Do not silently label these names LOCKED merely because they appeared in the latest proposal.

## 8.3 Legions, ORS, Sanctum Guard [LOCKED functional distinction]

- **Legions:** conventional professional military, armored infantry, drones, constructs, vehicles, electronic warfare, logistical formations, and specialized Resonant personnel.
- **ORS:** political intelligence, counterintelligence, surveillance, infiltrators, anti-Resonance enforcement, interrogations, clandestine operations. Not omniscient and not the entire army.
- **Sanctum Guard:** specialized elite protection of the Everflame's Core and sensitive technical installations; equipment and tactics minimize catastrophic collateral damage.
- **District Constabularies:** everyday policing and local enforcement, with Imperial backing.

All are subordinate to Seraphine; institutional rivalry does not create constitutional checks.

**WORKING ranks:** High Marshal, Marshal, Legate, Tribune, Centurion, Decurion, Legionary. **WORKING force estimates:** ~350,000 Legions; ~180,000 constabulary; ~35,000 ORS; ~2,000 Ascendancy combatants, excluding additional support and autonomous systems. These are provisional scale estimates, **not** audited or locked demographic facts.

## 8.4 Campaign power curve [LOCKED intent; WORKING encounters]

Early PCs should fear even limited Imperial deployments; a top Ascendant is not an ordinary early-game boss. Later PCs can challenge elite forces through alliances, terrain, technology, Resonance, infiltration, and preparation. Combat objectives can be escape, rescue, sabotage prevention, holding a line, or surviving, not always killing every enemy. Seraphine remains terrifying primarily through **strategic control**, not necessarily a high personal combat stat block.

# 9. The Cinder Network

## 9.1 One organized resistance [LOCKED]

There is **one organized underground resistance network**, the **Cinders / Cinder Network**, not a campaign full of competing rebel factions. It arose independently within Solmourn, principally in Frostward and Foundries with some Inner Wards support. Its members disagree on reform versus revolution, but they are one coordinated organization with decentralized cells.

It is neither automatically heroic nor the mandatory quest giver. It performs mutual aid, supply diversion, intelligence, clandestine communications, and occasional sabotage. It cannot defeat the Legions head-on or independently solve the Everflame. ORS infiltration and repression are real.

**WORKING size:** ~800 core operatives, ~12,000 supporters. **WORKING NPCs:** Mara Venn (coordinator/former Foundries logistics), Tarin Holt (Frostward courier/scout), Dr. Elara Senn (former thermal engineer). Elara can understand failing grid systems, **not Concordance**.

## 9.2 The forgotten external link [LOCKED]

User selected **A + B**: the Cinders were founded **entirely within Solmourn** and inherited an artifact of an older outside communications network.

The artifact, working name **Old Relay / Relay Nine**, was originally part of pre-isolation inter-Ember communications. It was decommissioned around **50 AS**, salvaged around **430 AS**, and eventually passed into underground hands. A Cinder Foundries workshop has reused components for **local encrypted communications**. Long-range capability is dormant; documentation is fragmentary.

The Cinders **do not know** the other Embers exist. The device is **not** a Concordance device, a readable map of nine cities, or a stored explanation of the natural sun.

**Possible play:** Cinder-aligned PCs may encounter the relay around sessions 4–5 without understanding its significance. After the sessions 8–9 map reveal, they may recognize routing symbols. Sessions 10–12 could include an **optional, difficult** repair/contact attempt requiring parts, engineering, access, and a receiving counterpart. It may fail; it never guarantees instant Vesperdeep contact, reinforcements, or a diplomatic rescue. Non-Cinder PCs must have independent paths to the central revelations.

# 10. The 15-session campaign architecture

## 10.1 Structure [LOCKED]

- **Exactly 15 sessions.**
- **Branching narrative** (option B), not linear cinematic rails or unrestricted sandbox.
- The crisis is real and progresses, but no scene assumes the PCs necessarily join a faction or make a particular moral choice.
- Combat and exploration are major pillars; political choices create stakes rather than replacing action.
- Key revelation timing is a **default target**, not an excuse to negate clever player action.

## 10.2 Established anchor events [LOCKED]

1. **Sessions 1–3:** Frostward heat failures, missing workers, early investigation, repression, and hints of underground aid.
2. **Sessions 4–5:** Deeper infrastructure exploration, increasing grid crisis, optional Cinder contact and Old Relay exposure.
3. **Session 6 — The Mercy Decree:** Seraphine orders a heat disconnection affecting approximately **84,000 Frostward residents** to stabilize the grid. The engineering threat is genuine; evacuation provision is inadequate; privileged districts remain protected. This is a pivotal moral and political event.
4. **If PCs openly defy the Mercy Decree:** user selected **A** — they become **immediate enemies of the state**, facing serious ORS/Legion pursuit and potentially Ascendancy intervention. Authorities may punish **proven or suspected collaborators**, with uncertainty and consequences. Do **not** automatically brand compliant, covert, or negotiating PCs as wanted.
5. **Sessions 7–9:** Dense urban Frostward around Mahwah/Ramapo; ancient buried archive exploration. **Sessions 8–9** reveal the historical map of **nine Embers** and pre-Sundering geography, including the New York identity. # 14. Tone, antagonists, and encounter ecology

## 14.1 Tone [LOCKED]

The city should feel immense, inhabited, sophisticated, and frightened—not an empty ruin. Its political terror is credible, systemic, and occasionally personal. Seraphine is the scariest figure in the city because of competence, absolute authority, and willingness to act, not because she constantly screams or kills randomly.

## 14.2 Enemy ecology [LOCKED]

Primary opponents and hazards include humans (constabularies, Legions, ORS, Ascendants, criminals, opportunists), autonomous machines/constructs, altered surviving wildlife in plausible protected environments, and dangerous infrastructure. **Do not default to Dissonance demons, cosmic horror monsters, undead hordes, or fantasy races.** Combat should arise naturally from the setting.

## 14.3 Agency [LOCKED]

No faction should automatically be entirely right or entirely evil in every individual case. The Imperium as a system is oppressive; individuals can have mixed motives. Cinders may take morally difficult actions. Players can resist, negotiate, exploit institutions, remain unaffiliated, or take unforeseen routes. The DM responds honestly to those choices.

# 19. One-paragraph setting synopsis (DM-facing)

Seven centuries after the Sundering severed the natural sun's warmth from Earth, 54 million people endure beneath the Everflame, an artificial sun above the ruins of New York City. The Solar Imperium's terrifying Empress Seraphine I Vael rules an unequal, technologically formidable megacity of six rings, where failing heat infrastructure threatens the overcrowded Frostward first. A single local resistance network, the Cinders, struggles against surveillance and calculated repression without knowing that eight other artificial-sun cities once existed. The Vigil's forgotten nine-rayed symbol quietly preserves that truth. As a Mercy Decree endangers tens of thousands and a buried archive reveals Earth's lost geography, the party may uncover evidence of a completely forgotten collective Resonance phenomenon that originally lit the nine Embers. The players—not prophecy, an all-knowing scholar, a rebel NPC, or a predetermined script—must decide whether Solmourn survives, what survival costs, and who controls its light.

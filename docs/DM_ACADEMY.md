# Solmourn DM Academy

> **DM ONLY — contains campaign spoilers.** Generated from the app seed; edit the seed, not this file.


## 1. Your job in one page (3 min)

*Present a situation, ask what they do, describe what changes.*

You are not writing a novel for your friends to act out. You present a situation, ask what their characters do, describe how the world responds, and apply fair rules when the outcome is uncertain.

In Solmourn that means making three things feel real: **the cold**, **the people**, and **the consequences of choices**.

**Three sentences that run a whole session**
1. "Here is what you see." (one sense, one moving detail)
2. "What do you do?"
3. "Here is what changes."

You may say "give me a moment". You may look things up. You may fix a mistake out loud. What players need is fairness, clear stakes and a world that reacts.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Say the three sentences aloud for this moment: the party stands outside Frostward Thermal Exchange 17 and hears four taps from beneath the street.

## 2. Session Zero and safety tools (10 min)

*Fifteen minutes that prevent most problems.*

**Ask the group (15 minutes):**
- **Tone:** grim, hopeful, political, frightening? What must never happen on screen?
- **Lines and veils:** lines are excluded entirely; veils happen off-screen. This campaign touches cold exposure, deprivation, surveillance, imprisonment, collective punishment, medical horror and children in danger. Ask about each.
- **Player conflict:** no attacks, theft or hostile checks between PCs unless everyone agrees.
- **House rules:** the six classes and Resonance Points are PLAYTEST. You may tune them between sessions, openly, never mid-fight.
- **Logistics:** session length, breaks, absences.

**The pause agreement:** anyone can ask to pause or reframe a scene without explaining why. Never penalize it. The cockpit's **Safety pause** button is there so you can do the same.

**Species (the Divergence):** any official D&D species is allowed. Ask each player for the book and version. Traits keep their printed mechanics; only the story explanation changes. Species never decides class, district or loyalty. If your table wants species prejudice in the story, agree on it here, with veils.

**Party glue:** every PC needs one link to another PC and a reason to be in Frostward during the first blackout. Nobody is required to like the Imperium or oppose it.

**Keep private:** your notes on anyone's lines and veils stay in DM-only fields. They are never exported.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Write your own two-sentence opening for Session Zero, then list the three topics you most expect your group to veil.

## 3. Session 1 rehearsal (one hour, optional second hour) (20 min)

*Walk through the first session before you run it.*

**Minute 0-10 - The blackout.** Read the opening aloud once, then stop. Ask each player: "Where are you, and whom are you trying to help?" Give every class one useful thing: the Warden holds a door, the Shade spots a hatch, the Machinist reads a gauge, the Votary warms someone, the Strider finds a route, the Resonant feels the field falter.

**Minute 10-35 - Three problems at once.** The lift, the clinic, the shelter doors. Say plainly that they cannot be everywhere. Whatever they leave gets worse; it is not lost. Mark their choice in **Choices**.

**Minute 35-60 - Mara's count.** The official number and the neighbours' number differ. A shift never came up. **This is your one-hour stopping point**: end on "So where do you start looking?"

**Optional second hour**
- **60-80 - Three leads.** Tarin the courier, the dispatch log, the frozen conduits. Give the lead for free. Rolls buy speed or detail only.
- **80-105 - The service door.** Glitching drones and venting steam. Talking, tools, stealth and force all work. Use **Initiative** only if weapons come out.
- **105-120 - The cordon.** Constables, then an ORS officer. End on the four taps from below.

**If it goes wrong**
- They ignore the plaza: the dark follows them. Ask what they hear.
- They fight the constables: the constables want order, not blood. Offer a way to stand down.
- Nobody talks: offer two concrete options and invite a third.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Set a timer for ten minutes and run the blackout scene alone, speaking aloud. Then name the one-hour stopping line you will use.

## 4. Calling for checks (6 min)

*Roll only when failure is interesting.*

1. **Ask intent and method.** "What are you trying to do, and how?"
2. **Decide if a roll is needed.** No roll for obvious facts, ordinary actions, or any clue the story needs.
3. **Pick ability, skill and DC.** 10 easy, 13 standard, 15 hard, 20 very hard.
4. **Roll** d20 + ability modifier + proficiency if proficient. Advantage: roll twice, keep the higher.
5. **Describe a concrete consequence.** Failure costs time, heat, trust or position. Never "nothing happens".

**Worked example - Exchange 17.** A player fails to read the dispatch log. They still learn the station number, because the campaign needs it. But the terminal flags the query, and ORS scrutiny may rise.

**Ring prejudice is a circumstance, not a penalty.** Outer-ring papers at a Crown checkpoint make some approaches harder and others easier. Do not apply a blanket minus.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Give a DC and a failure consequence for each: forcing a frozen hatch; convincing a constable to look away; reading an old gauge.

## 5. Adjudicating Resonance and overcasting (8 min)

*RP in, effect out, nothing spent until you confirm.*

**Basics (PLAYTEST).** A Pattern costs Resonance Points and still obeys action, range, concentration and level limits. One discipline per Resonant. RP replaces spell slots; never both.

**Use the assistant.** On a Resonant's sheet open **Patterns & overcasting**. It shows cost, strain and the save, and spends nothing until you tap Confirm.

**Amplified Overcasting:** twice per long rest, any Pattern, one bounded enhancement. Extra RP +2/+4/+6/+8, strain 1d6/2d6/4d6/6d6, CON save DC 12/15/18/21 or one exhaustion. Ask the player to name the target and the enhancement before any dice.

**Emergency Overcasting:** once per long rest, borrow up to 2 missing RP, 1d6 damage each, CON DC 10 + 2 per RP. Never together with Amplification.

**Say no clearly.** "No, a first-level Radiance Pattern cannot heat a district. It can tell you whether this regulator is drawing power."

**Resonance is watched.** Casting in front of constables or the ORS is a reason to move the ORS clock, and you should tell the players that is what happened.

**The lost art.** Nothing on any sheet is Concordance. If players ask whether working together does more, say "you could find out", and wait for sessions 10-12.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Open the assistant on a test Resonant: cast a level-2 Pattern with +2 Amplification and read the result aloud as you would at the table.

## 6. Running a fight (8 min)

*State the objective, announce who is next, keep it moving.*

**Before initiative:** say what the fight is about. "They want you out of the hall. You want the core." Most fights here have an objective other than defeating everyone.

**Each turn:** movement, one action, maybe a bonus action, one reaction per round. Say "Rin, you're up; Dax, you're next."

**Use the tracker:** Now → Initiative → add the party → add stat cards → roll → **Next turn**. Reactions reset automatically on each combatant's turn.

**Enemies are people or machines.** Constables retreat. ORS agents arrest. Legionaries follow orders. Constructs follow rules that can be exploited.

**Imperial power curve.** Early on, even a small Legion detachment is frightening and an Ascendant is a reason to run. Say so before the players commit: "You understand this is someone you cannot beat today."

**Too hard?** Add a believable exit: cover, an alarm that pulls enemies away, an enemy who would rather retreat. **Too easy?** Let them enjoy it. The app never changes a monster's HP by itself and neither should you, silently.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Run two rounds of the service-door drones against imaginary PCs using the tracker. Time yourself.

## 7. Exploration and hazards (7 min)

*Places with three ways through, and cold that matters without grinding.*

**Every site gets three things:** something to see, something that can go wrong, and more than one way through.

**Cold.** Treat it as a clock, not endless saving throws. "You have about four rounds of work before the people in the spur are in real danger." Reserve exhaustion for big failures.

**Hazard cards** (Library → Stat cards): steam breach, cold exposure, evacuation crush, Dissonance pocket. Each says what it does and how to deal with it.

**Bodies differ.** Flyers, swimmers and climbers use their printed speeds. Shafts, sealed tunnels, thermal doors and pressure seals create real choices, but never switch an ability off by fiat.

**Frostward is crowded.** Thirty million people of many species live there. Exploration there is stairwells, skyways, sub-basements and neighbours, never empty wilderness. The Wastes are somewhere else.

**Old-world fragments.** Drop one small unexplained remnant per site: lettering, a road shield, a sign. Describe it and move on. Do not explain.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Describe the flooded pump gallery in three sentences: one sense, one moving detail, one choice.

## 8. Spotlight rotation (4 min)

*Everyone acts before anyone acts twice.*

- After a big moment for one player, turn to another: "While that happens, what are you doing?"
- Combat and exploration lovers want things to **do**. Give each scene a physical problem as well as a conversation.
- If the party splits, cut between groups every few minutes on a small cliffhanger.
- If one player dominates: "Let's hear from someone who hasn't acted yet."
- Every class has a job in this city. Warden: hold a line. Shade: get in. Machinist: make it work. Resonant: sense and shape. Votary: keep people alive and calm. Strider: find the way.

**Rehearsal exercise:** For the hearing scene in session 5, write one prompt aimed at each of the six classes.

## 9. Clocks (5 min)

*Move them for reasons you can say out loud.*

Three clocks sit on the cockpit: **Everflame deterioration**, **ORS scrutiny**, **Public unrest**. Three more live under Campaign state.

- Move a clock only when something in the fiction justifies it. Never because time passed at the table.
- Tap **+**, then open the clock and write why. Undo is always there.
- Tell players the symptom, not the number: "There are two constables at the corner where there used to be none."
- Clocks can go down. A convincing cover story lowers scrutiny.

**Worked example - ORS scrutiny.** A Resonant casts in front of the ORS officer: +1. The party hands over the logs willingly: no change, or -1. Open defiance of the Mercy Decree: straight to 6.

Never use a hidden clock to force an ending.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Advance ORS scrutiny once in the app, write a reason, then undo it.

## 10. Fail-forward clues (6 min)

*Three routes to every truth; the lead is never gated by a roll.*

**Three-clue rule.** Each revelation in Mysteries has at least three clues from different sources: a person, an object, a place.

**Fail forward.** A failed roll still delivers the lead, at a cost: time, heat, damaged gear, attention.

**Two taps.** Now → Reveal clue → Mark found. Only the green player-safe line is shared; your private notes stay private.

**Truth versus belief.** Some residents believe in sabotage. They are wrong, and you should play them as sincere. Do not rewrite the truth because a player guessed something more exciting.

**Early discovery is a reward.** If they work something out ahead of schedule, let them.

**Worked example - the seven missing workers.** For where the workers went: the sign-in board (object), pipe tapping (place), neighbours who saw the vans (person). Four trapped, two detained, one deeper inside, and the party never has to find all of them. If they talk to nobody and read nothing, the frost trail is visible from the street.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Pick any revelation and say its three clues from memory. If you cannot, open it and add a route you would enjoy running.

## 11. Branching and improvising (7 min)

*Session numbers are pacing guides, not rails.*

When players go somewhere you did not plan:
- **Yes, and** - "That tunnel works; it is colder and slower."
- **Yes, but** - "You can bluff the constable. What papers are you showing?"
- **No, because** - and offer the nearest thing that could work.

**Record the choice.** Now → Choices lists the decision points for each session with what each option changes. Tap the one that happened and add a note. Nothing moves automatically.

**Worked example - the Mercy Decree.** Comply, act covertly, negotiate or defy. All four reach session 7. Only open defiance makes them wanted. Decide nothing for them, and do not brand the others as rebels.

**Worked example - the Old Relay.** If the party never meets the Cinders, skip it. They still reach every central truth. If they do find it, it is an old transmitter with strange marks, and nobody can tell them more.

**Do not protect a scene.** If a clever plan skips your favourite speech, move its information somewhere else.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Imagine the party hands the survey map to the Imperium in session 9. Write two sentences on what changes in session 10.

## 12. Handling the big reveals (7 min)

*Evidence first, then silence.*

**Before the reveal:** nothing about New York, other cities, or what lit the first suns appears in anything players can see. Use **Presenter mode** whenever the table can see your screen.

**The nine rays.** The Vigil's golden disk has nine rays. Mention it from session 1 as scenery and never explain it. If a player connects it to the map later, say nothing and smile.

**The map (sessions 8-9).** Let objects do the work: signage, the survey, the overlay. Read the overlay aloud, then stop talking. A roll can help interpret evidence; it never replaces it.

**What the map does not prove.** Nine lights existed long ago. It says nothing about whether any other still burns, and nothing about how they were made. Hold that line.

**The lost art (sessions 10-12).** Give observations, never a lecture. No NPC knows. If the players build a theory, write it in their words.

**The prophecy.** Its wording is undecided. Do not tell anyone they are chosen.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Read the overlay read-aloud from session 8 out loud, then sit in silence for ten full seconds. That is how long to wait at the table.

## 13. The finale (6 min)

*Costs on the table, no correct answer.*

- Present plans with prices: time, people at risk, materials, allies. Write them where everyone can see.
- Do not signal which ending you prefer. A restored sun is not automatically better than a repaired Everflame.
- Run three short linked challenges rather than one long battle: a decision, an engineering action, and a collective attempt if they earned one.
- Give each PC something only they can do. No caster is required.
- **Seraphine** is frightening because she is competent and absolute, not because she shouts. She may try to take the solution for House Vael. A fight with her is not assumed.
- Failure is allowed. It still gets an epilogue.
- End by asking each player for one image of the city a year later.

**Rehearsal exercise:** List three different endings your players might choose and one cost for each.

## 14. Twenty-minute prep and the ten-minute debrief (6 min)

*Prepare less than you think; record more than you think.*

**Before a session (5-10-5)**
- 5 min: reread last session's recap and what each NPC wants.
- 10 min: open the session's prep card; pick three likely scenes and the three clues that move the story.
- 5 min: check stat cards and Choices.

Stop when you have a first image, three clues, two approaches, NPC motives and stat cards.

**After a session (10 minutes)**
1. Ask "what are you doing next?"
2. Now → Recap: review the draft built from your notes.
3. Record invented facts as notes of type World fact.
4. Update clocks and write why.
5. Mark what was actually revealed.
6. Ask what was most fun and what dragged.
7. **Export a backup.**

The player recap export only ever contains text you approved.

**Rehearsal exercise:** Do the 5-10-5 for session 1 now, with a timer.

## 15. Quick reference card (2 min)

*The table-side essentials.*

- **DCs:** 10 easy · 13 standard · 15 hard · 20 very hard
- **Turn:** move, action, maybe bonus action, one reaction a round
- **Concentration:** one at a time; damage → CON save DC 10 or half damage
- **0 HP:** death saves; 3 successes stable, 3 failures dead, natural 20 = 1 HP
- **RP cost:** 0 / 2 / 3 / 5 / 6 / 7 / 9 / 10 / 11 / 13 for levels 0-9
- **Amplify:** +2/+4/+6/+8 RP · 1d6/2d6/4d6/6d6 · DC 12/15/18/21 · twice per long rest
- **Emergency:** borrow ≤2 RP · 1d6 each · DC 10 + 2 per RP · once per long rest
- **Don't know the rule?** "For now we'll rule X; I'll check after." Log it as a Rule call.
- **Never say yet:** the city's old name, other cities, what lit the suns, who is "chosen".

**Rehearsal exercise:** Keep this open on a second tap from Library during your first session.

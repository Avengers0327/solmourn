# SOLMOURN — EIGHT-SESSION DM ROADMAP

> **DM ONLY: campaign spoilers.** A practical, flexible guide to what to prepare, what to reveal, and how to keep choices meaningful. Use with `01_DM_STORY_BIBLE.md`.
>
> Assumptions: 3–5 level-3 human PCs at the start, approximately 2.5–3.5 hour sessions. **Adapt for actual group**; no specific party size or session duration has been confirmed. Session 1 also includes a 60-minute condensed option. Use milestone leveling; recommended levels are suggestions, not a rule.

## Campaign skeleton

**Promise:** Investigate a heat blackout; discover an aging Everflame, hidden history, the truth of Concordance, and options for humanity's future. **Principles:** secrets aren't character destinies; never require one successful roll for a clue; never require any player to be a caster; NPCs respond logically; all three final approaches remain playable.

### Before the first session — prep checklist

- [ ] Ask group size, session time, experience and boundaries; collect lines/veils.
- [ ] Give only `02_PLAYER_GUIDE.md` to players. Keep Story Bible and this roadmap private.
- [ ] Have each player create a level-3 human PC, with a link to another PC and a reason to be in Frostward.
- [ ] Confirm level-3 mechanics for selected **playtest classes**; approve ten legal Patterns for any Resonant.
- [ ] Print/save an **index card** for Mara, Tomas, Joren, Seren. Give each one clear want/fear/knowledge.
- [ ] Choose an opening scene location (Frostward heating queue, maintenance shift change, public shelter, or ration distribution).
- [ ] Decide what happened to 43 missing people—**must be more specific before running session 1**. Recommended: evacuated into a sealed maintenance spur after an automated emergency lockout; some are trapped and rescueable, not all dead. Avoid making their fate impossible to discover.
- [ ] Prepare 3 clues to the maintenance station, 2 possible routes in, 1 clock, 1 avoidable conflict, 1 meaningful choice.
- [ ] Have names for 5 improvised residents; track newly invented facts in the app/notes.
- [ ] Decide session length and scale scenes as needed.

## Session 1 — THE FIRST FLICKER

**Player-facing hook:** A district of Frostward loses its light for **17 minutes**. The heat loss is far worse than officials admit; **43 people are unaccounted for**. Mara Venn needs help finding her engineer brother Tomas.

**What is actually happening:** A degraded distribution regulator and emergency containment protocol reroute energy. The maintenance station locks down. A group of workers/residents end up isolated in a sealed service spur. Imperial messaging obscures the incident to prevent panic, but the Everflame itself has not been sabotaged.

**Opening read-aloud (optional, short):**

> The artificial daylight flickers. For a moment everyone in the queue falls quiet. Then the light goes out altogether. Somewhere beneath the street, a pump gives a shriek like torn metal. In the dark, you hear people counting aloud—trying to believe the warmth will return.

### 3-hour scene flow

| Time | Scene | Information / choice |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Blackout and immediate response | PCs can protect neighbors, stabilize equipment, organize shelter, or observe anomalies. Let different abilities matter. |
| 0:20–0:45 | Mara's plea / public rumor | Tomas missing; blackout lasted 17 min; heat authority's count differs from witnesses'. |
| 0:45–1:20 | Investigation: Joren, site perimeter, ration office | Three routes to maintenance station; avoid rolling for necessary basic directions. |
| 1:20–2:05 | Station access | Dialogue with guard, repair work, crawlspace, forged credentials, or stealth. Partial success has consequences, not dead ends. |
| 2:05–2:40 | Regulator chamber and trapped spur | Rescue challenge with degrading heat; choose between saving lives, copying diagnostic data, or pursuing fleeing suspicious official. |
| 2:40–3:00 | Consequence, Seren appears, cliffhanger | Confirm worn hardware, hint of mixed six-mode signal, new lead into buried infrastructure. |

### Three clue routes to the station

1. **Joren Pike** can identify the regulator's service entrance from shifts and maintenance sounds.
2. **Official dispatch log** lists a pressure-release anomaly at Station F-17 (find by persuasion, paperwork, theft, or access device).
3. **Physical evidence**: a sudden freezing pattern along thermal conduits leads toward the same subsystem.

**What to roll:** moderate check DCs **10 easy, 13 standard, 15 difficult**. Investigating basic obvious evidence needs no check. Failure costs time/heat/access or increases ORS interest, never deletes the station.

**Optional conflict:** 2–3 aggressive security drones or frightened civilians blocking access. Negotiate or bypass. For 4 level-3 PCs, start with published low-CR creature stats reskinned; tune after encounter calculator, don't invent high-damage exosuits.

**A concrete opening choice:** Rescue trapped residents first (lives secured, diagnostic data may degrade) or secure evidence first (more data, emergency clock advances). Permit inventive methods that achieve both at cost.

**60-minute cut:** Blackout (10m) → Mara/Joren (10m) → choose entry (10m) → regulator+rescue (25m) → Seren/cliffhanger (5m). **Do not force combat** in condensed version.

**End-state:** Party has at least one valid lead to older service tunnels, has met several consequential NPCs, and knows the blackout is a symptom of aging or management failure—not simply a monster attack.

## Session 2 — BENEATH THE ASHES

**Target level:** 3–4. **Question:** Who built the strange systems beneath the city, and why are some sections sealed?

**Opening:** Follow station diagnostics into archival maintenance vaults. The layers are older than the official city chronology suggests.

**Scene menu (choose 3–4):**
- Route through freezing maintenance tunnel with a heat-cell clock (collapsing insulation, steam, moving bulkhead).
- Cross-check logs: old **Form**, **Echo**, and **Kinesis** technology interfaces unexpectedly with a modern Radiance regulator.
- Encounter crew who want valuable components and distrust Imperial officers—social or tactical.
- Recover an archived schematic with six linked channel symbols and redacted author records.
- Optional Emergent Concordance *hint*, not forced trigger: multiple PCs stabilize a failing device together; describe harmonizing vibrations, not instant all-powerful magic.

**Essential clue:** An old system is designed to respond to coordinated **multiple disciplines**. Routes: schematic, technician, direct testing. **Cost:** opening a vault can draw ORS scrutiny.

**End with:** the problem extends beyond Frostward distribution; there is a systemic choice in how the city channels energy.

## Session 3 — THE PRICE OF DAYLIGHT

**Target level:** 4. **Question:** Who pays for the city's light?

**Scene menu:**
- Witness a ration council hearing: Crown, Gilded and Frostward all speak; none has complete information.
- Meet union technicians who can repair distribution but are prevented by restricted permissions.
- Follow the energy allocation ledger: investment in Crown aesthetics vs critical infrastructure.
- Observe local **Dissonance** near an overdriven Radiance-only transfer junction; a non-Radiance input reduces its instability.
- Present a genuine political choice: leak data, bargain with Seren, coordinate a lawful repair, or aid civil resistance.

**Three clues to unification:** old diagrams, technician anecdote, device behavior. **Avoid:** announcing Dissonance caused the Sundering this early.

**End with:** access to sealed historical infrastructure under the oldest parts of Solmourn, through whichever ally/route the PCs earned.

## Session 4 — THE CITY BEFORE

**Target level:** 4–5. **Major reveal:** Solmourn is built over **New York City**.

**Mystery structure:** Let players encounter an old subway/interchange, map, city name, skyline archive, or municipal utility code. Provide at least two independent confirmations. This is **not** a trivia check that requires knowing real-world New York.

**Set piece:** dormant transit hall, frozen platform, pre-Sundering museum/survey terminal. Play the gap between ancient bustling human city and current stratified survival state.

**Branching choices:** preserve the evidence, disclose it, bring it to a patron, or exploit it for access. Each changes ORS scrutiny and public rumors.

**Next geographic lead:** an archived **northern research/education corridor** points toward **Ramapo / Mahwah**. Do not assume the PCs know their modern geography: give a navigation chart, approximate distance and transport constraints in world terms.

**End with:** the city has a hidden former name; the northern sites may hold records the Imperium never reached.

## Session 5 — THE SEVENFOLD LIE

**Target level:** 5. **Major reveal:** Solmourn is **not** humanity's last city.

**Travel:** From the 16 km heated edge to the old Mahwah area, far beyond the perimeter. Treat cold as an environmental challenge, not endless CON-save punishment. Ensure means to prepare: vehicles, thermal packs, safe stations, guides, or recovered old tunnels.

**Travel clock:** four segments: (1) acquire permits/supplies, (2) cross thermal boundary, (3) navigate lost roads/river/terrain, (4) reach archive/relay approach. Complications: blizzard, energy-cell failure, ORS pursuit, scavengers, fragile bridge. Each has at least two solutions.

**Ramapo Ridge site:** An ordinary old school / educational archive survived in fragments. A student project's notes record anomalies in Resonance perception among people without known innate affinity. Treat these as evidence, **not immediate proof of the prophecy**.

**Other-city reveal routes:** world map with eight Core records; transregional power-standard markings; preserved broadcast; recovered expedition account. Even if school bypassed, put at least one route at the mountain relay.

**End with:** a detectable transregional signal, and moral dilemma: publish information, conceal it, or contact a stranger first.

## Session 6 — THE UNBOUND

**Target level:** 5–6. **Major reveal:** the First Concordance, non-innate learning, and the Sixth Canticle.

**Set piece:** ancient Ramapo Mountains relay—scientific control room, patchwork pre-Sundering network, unstable Resonance field, undeciphered six-mode diagnostics. It may communicate with **Vesperdeep** or another surviving city.

**Information ladder:**
1. Original Cores manifested during *simultaneous terror/despair*, not designed by a government.
2. Some original participants did not have innate Attunement.
3. The six disciplines share an underlying field; **Concordance** may be practiced deliberately at human scale.
4. A controversial record—Sixth Canticle—speaks of *one heart or many* becoming **Unbound**.

**Potential first Emergent Concordance:** a real life-or-death system failure with multiple possible solutions. If party pursues connection, offer the three-stage **Trial** (two checks DC 13 establish, DC 15 maintain, DC 16 resolve, damage on failed stages) or adaptive narrative alternative. It must **not be a mandatory gate**; a party that avoids the trial still gains the research and other rescue path.

**What they can earn:** source data / allies / a staged plan to learn controlled Concordance; **not** every character gaining all six disciplines instantly.

**End with:** a message from Solmourn—Everflame deterioration is accelerating, ORS/civil factions are moving, and return route choices matter.

## Session 7 — THE LAST PROCESSION

**Target level:** 6–7. **Question:** Who gets to choose the city's future?

**Return to Solmourn:** public procession, ration unrest, elite panic and workers' demands converge. NPCs react based on choices from earlier sessions. Seren can be obstacle/ally; Mara and Tomas have personal priorities.

**Three competing proposals:**
- Imperial emergency powers and exclusive repair access.
- Worker/community participation and shared infrastructure.
- External collaboration with other surviving cities, exposing state propaganda.

**Challenges:** get skilled workers/equipment, protect civilians from failing heating network, negotiate passage, halt accidental Dissonance cascade. The party can practice a **limited Controlled Concordance** if earned; it boosts diagnostics/stabilization, **not** full planetary sun restoration.

**End with:** quantified needs and consequences for each finale plan (time, people at risk, materials, political coalition). Players choose their direction.

## Session 8 — WHEN DARKNESS COMES

**Target level:** 7–8. **Question:** What will the world be allowed to become?

**Structure:**
1. **Recap the truth** and present actionable plans rather than one imposed 'correct' option.
2. **Pay costs**: assemble teams, obtain infrastructure, allocate heat, reassure or confront rulers, secure people in danger.
3. **Run the decisive technical/social challenge**, featuring different PC roles. A caster isn't required: Wardens protect, Shades acquire evidence/access, Machinists repair, Votaries coordinate medical/relief, Striders manage exterior routes, Resonants handle Patterns. Allies may provide missing disciplines.
4. **Concordance opportunity** if the group has learned it, optionally expressing Collective Unity; if not, use engineering/alliances and still allow a compelling ending.
5. **Epilogue**: who lives, who governs light, how other cities respond, what remains unknown.

**Outcomes (not rewards table):**
- **Everflame repair:** physically stabilize Solmourn; governance question remains.
- **Natural sun restoration:** immense, precarious boundary-reversal; not a single check or 9th-level Pattern.
- **Distributed dawn:** multiple heat/energy sources, freedom from centralized monopoly, prolonged implementation.
- **Hybrid:** temporary repair + slow transition.

**Avoid:** making sunrise automatically morally superior; making the group's previous choices moot; erasing consequences with deus ex machina.

**Rule:** Fail-forward means the PCs still get the *lead*, but perhaps at a cost (time, damaged gear, pursuit, compromised trust). It does **not** mean every action succeeds.

## Session-end 10-minute routine

1. Ask **what are you doing next?** (helps prep).
2. Record PC actions, newly invented NPCs, and changed allegiances.
3. Update *clue known/unseen* separately from *truth canon/working*.
4. Adjust three clocks based on specific events, and write why.
5. Write one evocative sensory description, one practical obstacle, one moral choice for next session.
6. End prep when you have the **first scene, three clues, two possible approaches, NPC motives, and stat blocks**. Avoid scripting dialogue for every route.

## Suggested milestone leveling

Start **3** → level **4** after session 2 → **5** after session 4 → **6** after session 6 → **7–8** during final sessions only if encounters/support rules can handle it. Alternative: remain 3–6 for a tighter 8-session campaign. **Do not jump levels simply because an app has a default schedule.**

## Hard consistency rules

- The catastrophe is **the Sundering**, not stylized mixed-case spelling.
- Eight suns manifested **simultaneously** from fear-and-despair-driven **Emergent Concordance**. They were not planned inventions.
- The natural sun survives behind a fictional planetary Dissonance effect.
- Everflame fails from physical age and engineering degradation.
- Some surviving cities are free; their original founders nevertheless became ruling elites.
- Eight-city collective name **not finalized**; do not say Pyres/Cinders as settled fact.
- NYC, other cities and the Unbound are spoilers. Keep them out of handouts for pre-reveal players.
- Concordance improves cooperation; the party never has to install one player as the story's only hero.
